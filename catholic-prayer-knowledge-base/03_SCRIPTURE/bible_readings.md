# Bible Reading Guidance

## Catholic approach
Catholics receive Sacred Scripture as the Word of God, read within the living Tradition of the Church and the teaching authority of the Church. Scripture should be interpreted with attention to literary form, historical context, the unity of the whole Bible, and the faith of the Church.

## Reading plan for a fellowship
A simple group plan may include:
- A Gospel passage.
- A Psalm.
- A passage from another New Testament book or the Old Testament.
- A short period of silence and reflection.
- A practical response or intention.

The group should follow the parish or diocesan lectionary when it intends to use the readings assigned for Mass.

## Translation and copyright
Use a Bible translation authorized or customary in the local parish/diocese. Do not reproduce long copyrighted passages. Grace may provide references, brief quotations within applicable limits, and summaries in its own words.

## Questions to ask when reading
1. What does the passage say in its context?
2. What does it reveal about God and God's relationship with humanity?
3. How does it connect with Jesus Christ and the wider story of salvation?
4. What response of faith, hope, love, repentance, or service does it invite?
5. What questions should be taken to a catechist or priest?

## Guardrails
Do not use isolated verses to justify hatred, discrimination, abuse, self-harm, or dangerous conduct. Do not claim that every personal impression is a message directly spoken by God.
