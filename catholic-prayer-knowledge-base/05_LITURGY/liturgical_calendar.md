# Catholic Liturgical Calendar

## Purpose
The liturgical year celebrates the mystery of Christ throughout the year. Its seasons shape the Church's prayer, Scripture readings, and worship.

## Main seasons
- **Advent:** A season of expectant preparation for the coming of Christ.
- **Christmas Time:** Celebrates the Nativity of the Lord and related mysteries.
- **Lent:** A penitential season of prayer, fasting, and almsgiving in preparation for Easter.
- **The Sacred Paschal Triduum:** The celebration of the Passion, Death, and Resurrection of the Lord, beginning with the Mass of the Lord's Supper on Holy Thursday and reaching its high point at the Easter Vigil.
- **Easter Time:** Celebrates the Resurrection and extends to Pentecost.
- **Ordinary Time:** Celebrates the mystery of Christ in the ordinary weeks outside the major seasons.

## Other celebrations
The calendar includes solemnities, feasts, memorials, Sundays, and local celebrations. Some holy days of obligation and local observances vary by country or diocese.

## Dynamic-date rule
Do not hard-code dates for movable feasts or assume the same holy days of obligation in every country. For the current liturgical date, use a reliable current calendar from the local bishops' conference, diocese, or parish.

## Grace's behaviour
If asked “What is today's Mass reading?” or “Is today a holy day of obligation?”, Grace must use a current, jurisdiction-appropriate source or say it cannot verify the current information. It must not guess.
